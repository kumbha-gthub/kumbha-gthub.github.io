Research Statement

My research lies at the intersection of scientific machine learning, signal processing, computer vision, and optimization. I am interested in developing machine learning algorithms that are not only accurate but also physically meaningful, interpretable, and robust under real-world operating conditions.

A major theme of my work is integrating physics-based knowledge with modern deep learning models to build reliable AI systems for scientific discovery and industrial applications. My long-term goal is to bridge theory and practice by designing learning algorithms that generalize well across changing environments while remaining computationally efficient.

Research Interests

  • Scientific Machine Learning Neural Operators, Physics-Informed Neural Networks, surrogate modeling, reduced-order modeling, scientific AI, and learning-based simulation.
  • Bayesian Optimization Gaussian Processes, batch Bayesian optimization, expensive black-box optimization, surrogate-assisted optimization, and uncertainty-aware learning.
  • Computer Vision & Perception Robust computer vision, representation learning, Vision Transformers, domain adaptation, anomaly detection, and learning under distribution shifts.
  • Machine Health Monitoring Condition monitoring, fault diagnosis, vibration signal analysis, predictive maintenance, and industrial AI.
  • Signal Processing Time-frequency analysis, image processing, audio processing, feature extraction, and deep learning for signals.
  • Deep Learning CNNs, Vision Transformers, Graph Neural Networks, GANs, Neural Operators, representation learning, and large-scale optimization.

Current Research

Efficient Batch Bayesian Optimization
2025–Present

Studying theoretical guarantees for pseudo-observation methods in batch Bayesian Optimization. My recent work introduces the notion of efficient conditioning, providing a theoretical explanation for why Gaussian-process-based batch BO methods produce diverse candidate sets while parametric surrogate models often collapse.

Fourier Neural Operators for Domain Adaptation
2025–Present

Developing U-FNO, a Fourier Neural Operator architecture for robust cross-domain fault diagnosis under sensor and operating-condition shifts using spectral learning and CORAL-based feature alignment.

Scientific Machine Learning for Engineering Design
2024–Present

Applying Neural Operators, surrogate modeling, and Bayesian optimization to accelerate engineering design optimization and reduce computational cost in industrial workflows.
